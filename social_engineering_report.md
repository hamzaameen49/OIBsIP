# Social Engineering Attacks Report

## Introduction
Social engineering is the art of manipulating people into giving up confidential information. It relies on human psychology rather than technical hacking skills. According to the 2023 Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report, over 70% of breaches involve the human element, making it one of the most effective attack vectors.

## 1. Phishing Attacks
**How it works:** Phishing involves sending fraudulent communications (usually emails) that appear to come from a reputable source, aiming to steal sensitive data like credit card numbers or login credentials.
**Types:** Spear phishing (targeted at specific individuals), Whaling (targeted at executives), Vishing (voice calls), and Smishing (SMS).
**Real-world Case Study:** In 2011, the RSA SecurID breach started with a spear-phishing email containing a malicious Excel file.
**Prevention Recommendations:**
1. Educate employees on identifying suspicious emails.
2. Implement email filtering and spam protection.
3. Use Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
4. Verify the sender's email address before clicking links.

## 2. Pretexting
**How it works:** An attacker creates a fabricated scenario (a pretext) to engage a victim and extract information. The attacker often impersonates a trusted figure, like an IT support agent or a police officer.
**Real-world Case Study:** The 2020 Twitter hack, where attackers used pretexting by calling Twitter employees and posing as IT staff to gain access to internal tools.
**Prevention Measures:**
1. Establish strict verification protocols for sensitive requests.
2. Train staff to question unusual requests, even if they appear urgent.
3. Implement internal controls requiring multiple approvals for critical actions.

3. Baiting
**How it works:** Baiting exploits human curiosity or greed. It often involves leaving physical media (like an infected USB drive) in a public place or offering fake downloads (like free music or movies).
**Real-world Case Study:** Various incidents where attackers dropped USB drives labeled "Payroll" or "Confidential" in company parking lots, which employees plugged into corporate computers.
**Prevention Measures:**
1. Ban unauthorized USB devices on corporate networks.
2. Disable autorun features on all computers.
3. Train employees never to plug in unknown devices.
4. Restrict downloads from untrusted sources.

Comparison Table
| Attack Type | Primary Target | Psychological Lever | Best Countermeasure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Phishing | General Users | Curiosity / Trust | Email Filtering & Training |
| Pretexting | Helpdesk / Employees | Authority / Urgency | Verification Protocols |
| Baiting | Curious Individuals | Greed / Curiosity | Endpoint Security Policies |

Organisational Recommendations 
1. Conduct regular security awareness training.
2. Simulate phishing attacks to test employees.
3. Establish a clear "Report Suspicious Activity" protocol.
4. Enforce MFA across all critical systems.
5. Keep software and systems up-to-date.
